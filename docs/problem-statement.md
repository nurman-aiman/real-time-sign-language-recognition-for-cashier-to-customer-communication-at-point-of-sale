# Problem Statement

## Who is affected?
The main users that affected are Deaf customers in Malaysia who use Malaysian Sign Language (BIM) when they try to shop and the cashier who serve them but do not understand (BIM). The other stakeholders that are included are shop owners, sign language interpreters, deaf associations and others.

## What exactly is going wrong?
Most of the cashier in Malaysia do not understand BIM and making it difficult for deaf individuals or customers to communicate their needs when shopping. According to Asia News Network (2025), Malaysia has only around 60 active sign language interpreters that serving more than 44,000 deaf people. This is the reason why most deaf customers having a hard time and  struggle to order and to ask about product prices, communicate between cashier on what they want to buy or what type of payment methods. Gopan (2025) also reported that 66% of hearing-impaired respondents found retail staff insufficiently trained to communicate with Deaf customers.

## Why does it matter?
This matter because there is a communication difficulties and barrier that can make shopping or ordering more challenging and hard for deaf customers. Without any suitable communication method for deaf individuals, they may need to rely on other people for help to guide them. A system that recognize selected BIM signs and it can displays the result as text to point of sale (POS) could help cashiers to understand customers requests and it will make a better shopping expeerience for deaf individuals.
